Solar water disinfection (SODIS) uses UV-A radiation and heat from sunlight to inactivate the pathogens that cause most common waterborne illness. It's been studied extensively for decentralized use in places without reliable water treatment infrastructure, and the method itself is simple enough to teach in a few minutes.

## The method

1. **Fill a clear PET bottle** about three-quarters full, shake it with the cap off for about 20 seconds to oxygenate the water (this measurably improves disinfection efficiency), then fill the rest of the way and seal.
2. **Lay it on a sun-exposed surface** — a roof, a sheet of corrugated metal, anywhere unshaded — for a minimum of **6 hours in full sun**, or **2 consecutive days if the sky is more than 50% cloudy**.
3. **Water is then safe to drink directly from the bottle.**

## What matters for this to actually work

- **Clarity of the water matters a lot.** SODIS works on relatively clear water — if turbidity is high (you can't read text through the bottle), pre-filter through cloth or let sediment settle and decant first, or the method won't reliably work.
- **Bottle material matters.** PET plastic transmits UV-A well; PVC does not, and glass blocks too much UV depending on type. Reusing common disposable water bottles is the standard, well-studied approach.
- **This does not remove chemical contaminants, heavy metals, or turbidity** — it's a biological disinfection method only. It's not a substitute for filtration if the water source has other contamination issues.

## Why this belongs in a community toolkit

It requires no electricity, no consumables beyond bottles you likely already have, and it scales linearly — one household or fifty, the method doesn't change. It's a good example of the kind of low-friction, no-single-point-of-failure infrastructure worth teaching widely rather than centralizing.

<div class="callout">
This is general, widely published information, not a substitute for local guidance on your specific water source. If you have reason to suspect chemical contamination, SODIS alone won't address it.
</div>
